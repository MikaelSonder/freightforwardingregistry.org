Freight Forwarding Registry is a structured editorial reference for freight forwarding across jurisdictions. It records the practical Customs, border, transport, carrier, documentation, permit, warehouse, delivery and cross-border context that can shape international and domestic freight movements.

The Registry is designed for importers, exporters, freight forwarders, logistics buyers, supply-chain teams, Customs and trade-compliance professionals, insurers, carriers, project owners and advisers who need a clear starting point for understanding the jurisdictional framework around ocean freight, air cargo, road, rail, intermodal, transit, Customs and final-mile logistics.

Registry Purpose

Primary FunctionTo provide a neutral, structured reference layer for freight forwarding and cargo movement processes across jurisdictions.
Typical CoverageOcean freight, air cargo, road, rail, intermodal and inland-waterway transport; Customs and border processes; Customs Brokers and representatives; EORI, CARM, CDS, eManifest, ICS2, NCTS and transit; dangerous goods; abnormal loads; port, airport, warehouse and bonded logistics; documentation; duties, taxes, origin, Incoterms® and cross-border delivery.
Use of RecordsEach record is designed to support shipment scoping, internal research, provider selection, route planning and jurisdictional navigation. It does not replace qualified Customs, transport, trade-compliance, tax, product, insurance, permit or legal advice.

Reference Coverage

Commercial Structure

Forwarder roles, carrier procurement, contractual versus intermediary services, quotations, tariff assumptions, Incoterms®, insurance, liability, claims and cost allocation.

Border and Customs

Importer/exporter roles, Customs representatives and brokers, EORI and equivalent identifiers, advance cargo data, declarations, transit, bonded procedures, duties, taxes, origin and release.

Transport and Delivery

Ocean, air, road, rail, inland waterway, RoRo and intermodal logistics; port and airport operations; warehouse and drayage coordination; route permits; dangerous goods; abnormal loads and final delivery.

Jurisdictions

Jurisdictional records describe freight forwarding within the relevant Customs, transport, border, carrier, documentation, permit, product-control and domestic-delivery context. Country records form the primary reference layer, with selected state, provincial and regional records providing additional local detail.

Country Records

Country-level records explain the principal Customs, border, logistics, documentation, process, risk and cross-border framework for freight forwarding in the relevant market.

Sub-Jurisdiction Records

State, provincial and regional records address local route, permit, port, airport, domestic-distribution, language and operational conditions that can materially affect cargo execution.

Regional References

European and international records provide cross-jurisdictional reference context. They complement, but do not replace, country-specific analysis.

Directory access: Browse all available country, state, provincial, regional and international reference records through the Jurisdictions directory.

Registry Method

Each record is organised as a jurisdictional reference rather than a provider listing or legal opinion. The record separates commercial forwarding activity from the distinct roles of importer, exporter, declarant, Customs representative, broker, carrier, warehouse operator, terminal operator, transit principal, insurer and national or local authority.

Object DefinitionDefines freight forwarding as a commercial transport-coordination and logistics service within the named jurisdiction.
Operational ScopeIdentifies relevant transport modes, gateways, domestic corridors, commercial responsibilities, provider boundaries and related service areas.
Authorities and FrameworksRecords the bodies, systems, rules and procedures likely to become relevant to a shipment, such as Customs, advance cargo reporting, transit, dangerous goods and route permits.
Process and DocumentsMaps the usual chain from shipment definition through booking, Customs and gateway release, inland dispatch, delivery, close-out and record retention.
Risk and Cost ContextHighlights common points of delay, cost exposure, documentation failure, insurance limitation, route constraint, border control and claims risk.

Use of Records

The Registry supports informed navigation and mandate design. It is particularly useful when a shipment involves a new country, a different Customs territory, a new carrier or gateway, cross-border road or rail, a new product category, bonded or transit procedures, temperature control, dangerous goods, abnormal loads, high value, time-critical delivery or complex domestic distribution.

Use CaseIdentify which jurisdictional factors should be established before a forwarder quotation, carrier booking, purchase order, sale contract, Customs declaration or delivery commitment is finalised.
Decision SupportCompare service scope, clarify party roles, identify Customs and filing dependencies, map required documents, anticipate route or permit issues and assign escalation responsibility.
BoundaryThe Registry does not determine a shipment’s Customs classification, origin, valuation, duty/tax outcome, product admissibility, permit eligibility, carrier liability, insurance coverage or legal position. Those matters require shipment-specific confirmation.
